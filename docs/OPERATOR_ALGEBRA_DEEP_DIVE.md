# A. Operator-algebra backbone: mathlib C⋆-algebras and the QuantumInfo migration

## A.1. Purpose and scope

This document is a design and salvage report for the operator-algebra work in
Physlib/QuantumMechanics/OperatorAlgebra, the older matrix-oriented
QuantumInfo tree, and the relevant parts of mathlib. It is intentionally
written as an engineering document: it identifies reusable infrastructure,
places where the current abstraction boundary is too narrow, likely theorem
homes, and a migration order that keeps finite-dimensional quantum mechanics
usable while the general theory is developed.

The central recommendation is to make operator algebra the public backbone of
physlib, with three deliberately connected layers:

    general C⋆-algebra A
            │
            ├── positivity, states, CP maps, CFC, GNS, dynamics, observables
            │
    Hilbert-space representation A → B(H)
            │
            ├── adjoints, vectors, rank-one operators, trace when finite-dimensional
            │
    finite-dimensional matrix / Hilbert model
            │
            └── matrices, density operators, Kraus/Choi, partial trace, entropy

The arrows should be expressed through constructions and equivalences, not by
making the general layer depend on matrices. Matrix theorems should be
corollaries or specialized implementations of the abstract API wherever the
mathematics genuinely supports that relationship.

## A.2. Executive recommendation

The existing direction is viable. The most valuable pieces already present are
the abstract observable/Jordan/Lie split, the GNS-based state infrastructure,
and the basis-free finite-dimensional density-operator representation. Those
should become the spine of the replacement.

The main architectural changes I recommend are:

1. Keep CStarAlgebra A as the minimal assumption for algebraic, star, norm,
   spectrum, CFC, and representation theorems.
2. Keep OperatorAlgebra A as a convenience bundle for the ordered physical
   layer, but do not require it in every theorem. In particular, order should
   be brought in only where a theorem actually talks about positivity or
   effects.
3. Treat A →ₚ[ℂ] ℂ as the canonical general state type. A state is a
   positive unital functional; density operators are its finite-dimensional
   representative, not its definition.
4. Treat A₁ →CP A₂ as the canonical general channel type. Trace-preserving
   terminology belongs to a setting equipped with a suitable trace or weight;
   in the Heisenberg picture, unital CP maps are the primary general notion.
5. Put Hilbert-space representation material in a middle layer around
   A →⋆ₐ[ℂ] (H →L[ℂ] H). It should expose vector states and rank-one
   operators without forcing every abstract theorem to mention H.
6. Re-home the proven finite-dimensional material from QuantumInfo behind
   the new general interfaces. Preserve matrix algorithms and finite formulas,
   but stop duplicating order, CFC, positivity, adjoints, and CP-map concepts
   that mathlib already supplies.
7. Make the picture convention explicit. The current abstract channel
   direction is naturally Heisenberg: a unital CP map sends observables
   forward. Schrödinger-picture state transport should be expressed by a
   dual/adjoint construction when a predual or finite trace pairing is
   available.

## A.3. The three levels and their responsibilities

### A.3.1. Level I: the general C⋆-algebra

This level should be usable for arbitrary unital complex C⋆-algebras and, when
appropriate, non-unital algebras. Its core subjects are:

- self-adjoint elements and real-linear observable spaces;
- positive elements, spectral order, square roots, powers, and functional
  calculus;
- positive and completely positive maps;
- states as positive unital functionals;
- representations, GNS, and abstract automorphisms;
- Jordan and Lie structures on self-adjoint elements;
- bounded one-parameter dynamics generated by self-adjoint elements;
- abstract effects, POVMs, and compatibility notions.

No matrix trace, determinant, eigenbasis, coordinate vector, Kraus list, or
finite cardinality should be required here. Some important physical subjects
are simply not available at this level without extra structure: density
matrices, partial trace, finite Shannon/von Neumann formulas, and a general
Schrödinger-picture trace-preserving condition.

### A.3.2. Level II: Hilbert-space representations

This level specializes to a representation of an algebra on a complex Hilbert
space, usually

    π : A →⋆ₐ[ℂ] (H →L[ℂ] H)

or a bundled representation structure that records nondegeneracy and
continuity as needed. It should contain:

- adjoints and unitary operators;
- conjugation of operators and the induced automorphism;
- vector and vector-mixture states;
- rank-one operators and projections;
- positivity as a quadratic-form statement;
- spectral and eigenspace facts when finite-dimensionality is assumed;
- the bridge from an abstract state to its GNS representation.

This is the right level for general Hilbert-space quantum mechanics. It still
does not automatically have a trace. A trace is available for finite-dimensional
spaces and for selected trace-class ideals, but not for every bounded operator
on every Hilbert space.

### A.3.3. Level III: finite-dimensional matrices

This level may assume a finite index type, finite-dimensional Hilbert space,
and the matrix/operator equivalence. It should support:

- Matrix n n ℂ and EuclideanSpace ℂ n operators;
- Hermitian matrices and positive semidefinite matrices;
- density operators and the finite trace pairing;
- Kraus and Choi representations;
- partial traces and tensor products;
- spectral decompositions and matrix functions;
- entropy, fidelity, distinguishability, channel capacity, and resource
  theories.

The finite layer is not a second definition of the theory. It is a rich model
where additional operations exist and many abstract statements become
computable.

## A.4. Mathlib survey: the abstraction boundary to build on

### A.4.1. C⋆-algebra classes

The central classes are in Mathlib.Analysis.CStarAlgebra.Classes and the
supporting norm/star results are in CStarAlgebra.Basic. CStarAlgebra A
already packages the complex normed algebra, star structure, completeness,
the C⋆ identity, and the required star-module/algebra compatibility.

This class intentionally does not impose an order. That is important: the
norm-star algebraic theory is broader than the ordered physical presentation,
and mathlib's continuous functional calculus has its own carefully chosen
assumptions.

The current declaration is approximately:

    class OperatorAlgebra (A : Type*) extends
      CStarAlgebra A, PartialOrder A, StarOrderedRing A

This is a useful application-level bundle, but it should not become the only
entry point. Recommended convention:

    -- algebraic theorem
    variable [CStarAlgebra A]

    -- positivity/order theorem
    variable [OperatorAlgebra A]

If future mathlib versions provide an ordered C⋆-algebra class or a more
focused typeclass, the physlib bundle should adapt to it rather than forcing a
parallel theory.

### A.4.2. Positive elements and spectral order

The most important order infrastructure is in the continuous-functional-
calculus files:

- Mathlib.Analysis.CStarAlgebra.ContinuousFunctionalCalculus.Basic;
- ...Order;
- ...Instances;
- ...Note.

The relevant constructions include CStarAlgebra.spectralOrder and
CStarAlgebra.spectralOrderedRing. Mathlib also has characterizations of
nonnegativity through square factors and spectrum, including the useful shape

    0 ≤ a ↔ ∃ x, x⋆ * x = a

under the appropriate assumptions, and results relating positivity to the
spectrum of self-adjoint elements.

This has two direct consequences for physlib:

1. Do not make HermitianMat or a new abstract positive-element wrapper
   re-prove the general square-root and spectral-order theory.
2. If a wrapper is useful for readability, define it as a thin subtype or
   notation over the existing self-adjoint/positive predicates and provide
   coercions and bridge lemmas.

The CFC API is especially valuable for observables. It gives square roots,
positive/negative parts, real powers, inverses under nonvanishing hypotheses,
and projections in the cases where the relevant continuous function exists.
It does not provide an automatic Borel functional calculus. Entropy's
x * log x at zero and support projections may need dedicated lemmas or a
finite-dimensional route.

### A.4.3. Positive and completely positive maps

Mathlib.Analysis.CStarAlgebra.PositiveLinearMap supplies positive linear maps
with notation such as

    A₁ →ₚ[ℂ] A₂

and the expected positivity, boundedness, and continuity results. This should
be the source of truth for states and positive observables.

Mathlib.Analysis.CStarAlgebra.CompletelyPositiveMap supplies completely
positive maps, written in the current code as

    A₁ →CP A₂

The definition uses finite matrix amplifications through CStarMatrix. This is
exactly the generalization needed for channels: the domain and codomain need
not be matrices, while complete positivity still quantifies over all finite
amplifications.

Important consequences:

- a star algebra homomorphism is already completely positive through the
  relevant class instance;
- CP maps have order-preserving behavior;
- map_cstarMatrix_nonneg is the key amplification theorem;
- unital CP maps are the natural Heisenberg channels;
- finite-dimensional Kraus/Choi theorems should be proved later as
  representation theorems, not used to define CP.

The current Channel abbreviation,

    {φ : A₁ →CP A₂ // φ 1 = 1}

is therefore a good starting point. It should eventually receive a named
structure, because the named structure can carry picture/convention metadata,
composition, identity, and later continuity or normality assumptions without
breaking every downstream definition.

### A.4.4. CStarMatrix and amplification

Mathlib.Analysis.CStarAlgebra.CStarMatrix is a major bridge. It packages
finite matrices over a C⋆-algebra and carries the operator norm, star, square
C⋆-algebra structure, and order infrastructure needed by complete positivity.

This gives the right conceptual separation:

    CP(A, B) is abstract
    CP tests use CStarMatrix n n A and CStarMatrix n n B
    matrix/Kraus formulas are only one finite representation

The QuantumInfo channel code currently hardcodes Kronecker products and matrix
cones in its CP predicate. That code is valuable as a finite proof laboratory,
but its public predicates should migrate to the mathlib CP map notion. The old
Choi/Kraus theorems can then be restated as equivalences for finite matrix
algebras.

### A.4.5. Hilbert-space operators and adjoints

The key files are:

- Mathlib.Analysis.CStarAlgebra.ContinuousLinearMap;
- Mathlib.Analysis.InnerProductSpace.Adjoint;
- Mathlib.Analysis.InnerProductSpace.Positive;
- Mathlib.Analysis.InnerProductSpace.StarOrder;
- Mathlib.Algebra.Star.UnitaryStarAlgAut.

For a complete complex inner-product space, bounded continuous linear maps
H →L[ℂ] H form a C⋆-algebra. Mathlib supplies adjoints, unitary operators,
unitary conjugation, positivity via quadratic forms, and star algebra
equivalences induced by isometries.

The Automorphism.lean work in this repository correctly exposes an important
scope distinction:

- in any unital C⋆-algebra, a unitary gives an inner automorphism;
- for B(H), suitable automorphisms are spatial/unitarily implemented;
- this does not mean every automorphism of an arbitrary C⋆-algebra is inner.

The projective-unitary quotient is a useful finite/Hilbert-space construction,
but it belongs below the abstract automorphism API. The scalar phase kernel is
an implementation detail of the quotient equivalence, not a general
C⋆-algebra fact.

### A.4.6. Exponentials and bounded dynamics

Mathlib.Analysis.CStarAlgebra.Exponential provides the crucial theorem that
the exponential of a self-adjoint element is unitary. The current
unitaryEvolution and hamiltonianFlow direction is therefore mathematically
well chosen for bounded Hamiltonians:

    U t = expUnitary (t • H)
    α t (a) = U t * a * U t⋆

The general layer should state group and automorphism properties first, with
the differential Heisenberg equation as a separate theorem requiring the
analytic hypotheses needed for differentiation. Unbounded Hamiltonians,
self-adjoint operators with domains, strongly continuous unitary groups, and
Stone's theorem are a separate future subsystem; they should not be silently
represented by bounded operators.

### A.4.7. GNS and abstract states

Mathlib.Analysis.CStarAlgebra.GelfandNaimarkSegal already supports the bridge
from a positive functional to a Hilbert-space representation. The current
State.lean uses PositiveLinearMap.toPreGNS and preGNS_inner_def to derive an
abstract Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and uncertainty relations.

This is one of the strongest existing foundations in the repository. Preserve
it and make it central. A state should expose:

    State A := { φ : A →ₚ[ℂ] ℂ // φ 1 = 1 }

or an equivalent named structure. From it, the library can derive a GNS
representation, a cyclic vector, and vector-state formulas. This avoids
requiring a preferred Hilbert space for the abstract theory.

### A.4.8. Matrices, Hermitian structure, and finite order

Mathlib has two relevant finite routes:

- Analysis.CStarAlgebra.Matrix gives matrices a C⋆-algebra under the scoped
  Matrix.Norms.L2Operator norm;
- Analysis.Matrix.Order gives Loewner order and positive-semidefinite
  characterizations under the MatrixOrder scope.

LinearAlgebra.Matrix.Hermitian and Analysis.Matrix.PosDef provide the
Hermitian and positive-semidefinite vocabulary. Analysis.InnerProductSpace.Trace
provides the operator trace route, while adjoint files supply matrix/operator
conversion lemmas such as the orthonormal-basis matrix representation.

The repository's finite matrix layer should standardize its scopes and
instances around these APIs. Installing global partial-order instances for
matrices and bounded operators is risky because the same type can acquire
competing order instances from scoped mathlib constructions. Prefer explicit
scopes or a dedicated wrapper when instance coherence cannot be guaranteed.

## A.5. Audit of the current OperatorAlgebra tree

### A.5.1. Basic.lean

The file has a sensible vocabulary: Observable, PositiveElement, Effect,
POVM, Unitary, State, Channel, and Representation. The next step is to turn
these abbreviations into a stable public API with named structures where
composition and laws matter.

Recommended changes over time:

- keep Observable A := selfAdjoint A as a lightweight alias;
- use existing positivity predicates and CFC rather than a parallel cone;
- make State a named subtype/structure of positive unital functionals;
- make Channel a named unital CP map structure;
- separate Representation from a bare star algebra hom if nondegeneracy or
  continuity will matter;
- state whether POVMs are finite, countable, or finitely additive. The current
  sum definition is specifically finite and should say so in its name or
  documentation.

The OperatorAlgebra class itself should remain a convenience layer. A raw
C⋆-algebra can support states, CP maps, CFC, and GNS without necessarily
having the project-wide order instance that the bundle introduces.

### A.5.2. Jordan.lean

The Jordan product on self-adjoint elements is a good general construction:

    a ∘ b = Re(a b) = (a b + b a) / 2

The current wrapper JordanObservable is the right response to the fact that
the ambient self-adjoint subtype already has a multiplication story that can
conflict with a new global product instance. Keep the wrapper and document the
coercion lemmas prominently.

Useful next theorems are positivity-preservation statements for squares,

    a ∘ a = a²,

commuting-observable compatibility with ordinary multiplication, and the
relationship between Jordan powers and CFC for a single observable.

### A.5.3. Lie.lean

The Lie product is the imaginary/commutator component on self-adjoint
observables. This is the correct companion to the Jordan product. Keep both
real structures in the abstract layer and prove the associative-to-Jordan/Lie
compatibility identities there.

The current qubit-specific Lie file is out of sync with the abstract API: the
build reports references to OperatorAlgebra.Observable.obsBracket and
bracket_def that are no longer available under those names. This is a
downstream naming problem, not evidence against the general construction. A
small compatibility/renaming pass should repair it after the report.

### A.5.4. Observable.lean

The real-basis and coordinate-equivalence results are useful for finite
applications, but the abstract observable space should remain a real vector
space with no preferred basis. Basis selection belongs in finite-dimensional
modules and concrete models.

This split is important for GNS and representation-independent statements:
expectation values, variance, commutation, and positivity should not depend on
coordinates.

### A.5.5. State.lean

This is currently the strongest abstract physics file. It defines expectation,
centered observables, variance/covariance, Cauchy–Schwarz, and
Robertson–Schrödinger-style uncertainty relations, and it uses the GNS inner
product rather than a finite matrix trace.

Salvage and expansion priorities:

- expose a reusable state structure instead of making all results depend on a
  local state : A →ₚ[ℂ] ℂ plus a normalization hypothesis;
- add expectation positivity and norm bounds;
- define state composition with a representation/vector state;
- separate the symmetric Jordan covariance from the antisymmetric Lie term;
- add a variance-zero characterization modulo the GNS null space;
- later add state pullback along a unital CP map.

### A.5.6. FiniteDim/Matrix.lean

The matrix/operator equivalence is exactly the right finite bridge:

    Matrix n n ℂ ≃⋆ₐ[ℝ]
      (EuclideanSpace ℂ n →L[ℂ] EuclideanSpace ℂ n)

It should be treated as a canonical implementation theorem, with all later
finite state/channel results able to move between matrices and operators.

The main audit item is instance management. The file currently installs
partial-order and star-ordered-ring instances for matrices and continuous
linear maps. Compare these carefully with mathlib's scoped MatrixOrder
instances before making them global in the public import path.

### A.5.7. FiniteDim/StateRepresentation.lean

This file is the key bridge to preserve. It already develops a basis-free
finite-dimensional representation of states by density operators using the
operator trace pairing. It contains the ingredients needed for:

- the finite-dimensional Riesz representation of a positive state;
- the equivalence between normalized positive functionals and density
  operators;
- vector states and mixtures;
- pure-state/rank-one constructions;
- compactness and convexity of the state space;
- spectral decomposition.

The recommended public theorem is an equivalence of state spaces, conceptually

    State (H →L[ℂ] H) ≃ DensityOperator H

for finite-dimensional H, with a second theorem transporting this through
Matrix.toEuclideanCLM. This should become the canonical replacement for
QuantumInfo.States.Mixed.MState, while retaining an adapter for old code.

### A.5.8. Automorphism.lean

The file correctly uses mathlib's unitary-conjugation and spatial-equivalence
results. It should be generalized in two directions:

- abstract inner automorphism from a unitary in any unital C⋆-algebra;
- Hilbert-space spatial implementation as a separate theorem/module.

The file also gives a natural route to show that *-automorphisms are unital CP
maps. That should be stated once and reused by dynamics and channels.

### A.5.9. Dynamics.lean

The bounded Hamiltonian flow is a good Level I/II feature. The current build
has one mathlib API mismatch: mem_ker_conjStarAlgAut_iff is not present under
that name. The surrounding proof should use the existing unitary conjugation
extensionality/kernel theorem, likely Unitary.conjStarAlgAut_ext_iff' or its
exact current replacement.

The file should avoid making derivative statements look unconditional. A
clean split is:

1. algebraic unitary group and automorphism laws;
2. norm-continuity/strong continuity;
3. derivative and Heisenberg equation;
4. finite matrix exponential formulas.

## A.6. What to salvage from QuantumInfo

### A.6.1. Keep as finite applications or adapters

The following areas contain valuable mathematics and should survive, but their
foundational predicates should move onto the new API:

- mixed states and density operators;
- pure states, kets, bras, and rank-one operators;
- POVMs and finite measurements;
- finite channels, Kraus decompositions, Choi matrices, and dual maps;
- tensor products, product states, partial traces, and purification;
- fidelity, trace distance, Schatten norms, and matrix inequalities;
- von Neumann, Rényi, and relative entropy;
- finite resource theories and capacity results once their proofs are solid.

QuantumInfo/ForMathlib/HermitianMat is particularly rich. It contains finite
CFC, order, inner products, traces, projections, powers, logarithms, Schatten
quantities, and partial traces. Much of it is not disposable; it is a source
of finite theorem statements and proof patterns. The wrapper itself should be
reduced where mathlib now supplies the same theorem for selfAdjoint or
positive elements.

### A.6.2. MState

QuantumInfo/States/Mixed/MState.lean is the obvious compatibility target. Its
public concepts map as follows:

| QuantumInfo concept | Replacement home |
| --- | --- |
| MState | finite DensityOperator / normalized positive functional |
| exp_val | abstract expectation specialized to a density state |
| pure | vector state or rank-one density operator |
| spectralDecomposition | finite CFC/spectral theorem layer |
| traceLeft, traceRight | finite tensor-product/partial-trace layer |
| product state | tensor-product state layer |
| purification | finite Hilbert representation layer |
| convexity | general state convexity, finite compactness specialization |
| traceNorm_eq_one | normalized finite density-operator theorem |

The existing file's pos : 0 < ρ.M deserves an audit. In an ordered matrix
type, strict positivity can mean merely nonzero positive semidefinite, whereas
“positive definite” is stronger. The replacement should use names that
distinguish PositiveSemidefinite, PositiveDefinite, and Nonzero.

### A.6.3. Pure-state braket code

QuantumInfo/States/Pure/Braket.lean has useful coordinate-level ket/bra and
phase results. It should sit in the finite Hilbert layer and be implemented in
terms of vectors and continuous linear functionals where possible. The phase
quotient and ray language is reusable, but a general “pure state” should be a
state admitting a vector-state representation, not a coordinate vector by
definition.

### A.6.4. POVMs and measurements

QuantumInfo/Measurements/POVM.lean has a clear finite target: effects are
positive operators bounded above by one, and a finite POVM is a finite family
whose sum is one. The abstract Effect and POVM declarations in Basic.lean
are the right destination.

The migration should first define the finite POVM as an instance of the
abstract finite POVM, then retain matrix probability and measurement-update
lemmas as finite theorems. Infinite outcome spaces should be deferred until a
measure/integration design exists.

### A.6.5. Channels

The bundled channel hierarchy in QuantumInfo/Channels/Bundled.lean is useful
as a taxonomy, but several names conflate different pictures:

    HP, positive, CP       algebraic positivity properties
    unital                 Heisenberg normalization
    TP                     Schrödinger trace normalization
    CPTP                   finite trace-paired combination
    Kraus / Choi            finite representation theorems

The replacement should define CPMap from mathlib and make UnitalCPMap the
abstract channel. In the finite trace-paired layer, define a dual map and prove
that unitality of one side corresponds to trace preservation of the other.
Then re-express the old CPTPMap as a finite alias/structure with explicit
picture documentation.

QuantumInfo/Channels/Unbundled.lean is a useful theorem inventory but its CP
predicate hardcodes matrices and Kronecker amplifications. Salvage theorem
statements and examples; replace the primitive predicate.

### A.6.6. Dual maps

QuantumInfo/Channels/Dual.lean contains an important physical bridge. The
abstract replacement should not pretend every bounded map between C⋆-algebras
has a trace adjoint or predual. Instead:

- define composition/pullback of states along unital positive/CP maps
  abstractly;
- define trace adjoints in finite-dimensional trace-paired modules;
- prove the unital/trace-preserving duality there;
- later add normal maps and preduals if von Neumann algebra support is desired.

This distinction prevents finite-dimensional convenience from leaking into the
general C⋆-algebra API.

### A.6.7. Entropy and information theory

The entropy tree is a high-value downstream client, not a foundation. Its
finite definitions depend on trace, spectrum, logarithm, tensor products, and
partial trace. The migration order should be:

1. stable finite density operators and finite CFC/logarithm;
2. trace and Schatten API;
3. scalar and operator entropy formulas;
4. states/channels and data processing;
5. tensor-product entropy identities;
6. capacities and resource theories.

The current tree contains unfinished or axiomatised portions, including
capacity files and some entropy/information inequalities. They should be
labelled as downstream work rather than used as evidence that the replacement
foundation is complete.

## A.7. Proposed module architecture

The following is a target organization, not a demand for a single refactor
commit.

    Physlib/QuantumMechanics/OperatorAlgebra/
      Basic.lean                         vocabulary and minimal bundles
      Observable.lean                    self-adjoint real space
      Positive.lean                      effects, positive parts, CFC bridges
      Jordan.lean                        Jordan product
      Lie.lean                           Lie product and commutator
      State.lean                         positive unital functionals
      State/GNS.lean                     GNS and vector-state bridge
      Channel.lean                       CP maps and unital channels
      POVM.lean                          finite/abstract outcome APIs
      Automorphism.lean                  inner and spatial automorphisms
      Dynamics.lean                      bounded one-parameter flows
      Representation.lean                representations on Hilbert spaces
      Hilbert.lean                       B(H), vectors, rank one, projections
      FiniteDim/
        Matrix.lean                      matrix/operator equivalences
        StateRepresentation.lean         density-state equivalence

        Entropy.lean                     finite entropy layer

The existing FiniteDim/StateRepresentation.lean can remain in place and
become the first mature finite module. The exact file split can be adjusted to
avoid churn; the conceptual dependency direction is the important part.

## A.8. Suggested core interfaces

### A.8.1. State

Use a named structure so normalization is not repeatedly passed as a theorem
hypothesis:

    structure State (A : Type*) [NonUnitalCStarAlgebra A] where
      functional : A →ₚ[ℂ] ℂ
      normalized : functional 1 = 1

The exact class assumptions should follow the available positive-map API. If
non-unital states require approximate units or a different normalization, make
the unital state a separate structure rather than weakening the semantics of
the word state.

Derived operations:

    def State.expect (ω : State A) : Observable A →ₗ[ℝ] ℝ
    def State.pullback (ω : State B) (Φ : A →CP B) (hΦ : Φ 1 = 1) : State A
    def State.gns (ω : State A) : Representation A (GNSHilbertSpace ω)

The pullback direction makes the Heisenberg convention explicit: an observable
map Φ : A → B transports a state on B back to a state on A.

### A.8.2. Channel

Conceptually:

    structure UnitalCPMap (A B : Type*) [CStarAlgebra A] [CStarAlgebra B]
        extends A →CP B where
      map_one' : toCompletelyPositiveMap 1 = 1

Composition, identity, and adjoint/pullback-of-states should be the first
instances/theorems. Add “normal”, “trace-preserving”, or “finite-dimensional”
only in modules that have the needed extra structure.

### A.8.3. Observable dynamics

The abstract bounded construction should look like:

    def innerAutomorphism (u : Aˣ) (hu : IsUnitary u) : A →⋆ₐ[ℂ] A
    def hamiltonianFlow (H : Observable A) : ℝ → AutomorphismGroup A

The exact unitary type may use mathlib's existing Unitary A rather than a
new subtype. Prove the group law, phase invariance, and compatibility with
states before adding derivatives.

### A.8.4. Finite density operator bridge

For finite-dimensional H, define density operators as positive self-adjoint
operators with trace one, then prove:

    normalized positive functionals on B(H)
        ≃ density operators on H

The existing operator trace pairing and separating/nondegeneracy results are
the correct route. A matrix specialization should be a transport theorem,
not the primary proof.

## A.9. Migration plan

### A.9.1. Phase 0: stabilize conventions and instances

- Decide and document that abstract channels are Heisenberg unital CP maps.
- Decide which definitions require CStarAlgebra and which require the ordered
  OperatorAlgebra bundle.
- Audit global matrix/operator order instances against mathlib scoped order.
- Give State, Channel, Effect, and POVM stable names/docstrings.
- Do not add new matrix-only foundational predicates.

### A.9.2. Phase 1: complete the abstract backbone

- finish the state structure and state pullback;
- move positive-element and effect lemmas onto CFC/order APIs;
- add the CP-map channel structure and composition laws;
- expose GNS and vector-state bridges;
- repair the qubit Lie downstream naming mismatch;
- repair the dynamics kernel theorem against the current mathlib API.

### A.9.3. Phase 2: make the finite bridge canonical

- stabilize FiniteDim/Matrix.lean and its scopes;
- finish the density-operator/state equivalence;
- add matrix/operator transport lemmas;
- define finite trace adjoints and the unital/trace-preserving duality;
- provide adapters from MState to the new density/state types.

### A.9.4. Phase 3: move measurements, channels, and tensor products

- re-express finite POVMs through abstract effects;
- re-express finite CP maps through mathlib CP maps;
- prove Kraus and Choi equivalences in the finite matrix layer;
- port partial trace, product states, and purification after tensor conventions
  are fixed;
- keep old QuantumInfo names as deprecated adapters during the transition.

### A.9.5. Phase 4: port analysis and information theory

- port fidelity, trace distance, Schatten results;
- port entropy and relative entropy;
- port data-processing inequalities and monotonicity;
- port resource theory and capacities only after removing unfinished proofs or
  clearly marking them as future work.

### A.9.6. Phase 5: deprecate the old tree

Once the finite bridges and adapters are used by downstream modules:

- mark QuantumInfo foundational types deprecated;
- stop adding new primitives there;
- leave finite application modules temporarily if they are still useful;
- remove duplicate order/CFC/positivity definitions only after all clients have
  migrated and the build confirms no hidden dependency.

## A.10. Major risks and design decisions

### A.10.1. Order-instance coherence

This is the immediate technical risk. Matrix order is naturally scoped in
mathlib, while project files currently create instances for matrices and
continuous linear maps. A global instance can make unrelated imports fail or
silently select an unintended order. Prefer:

- mathlib's established scoped order where possible;
- wrapper types only when two mathematically different orders must coexist;
- localized letI/attribute local instance for proof-specific needs;
- a single documented project-level instance only after a full import audit.

### A.10.2. Positivity versus positive definiteness

Quantum information uses “positive” in at least three senses: positive
semidefinite operator, strictly positive/positive definite operator, and a
nonzero positive operator. The API must distinguish them in names and theorem
statements. This is especially important when porting MState.pos and support
projection results.

### A.10.3. Trace is not general C⋆-algebra structure

Do not put trace : A → ℂ into the general OperatorAlgebra class. A generic
C⋆-algebra may have no canonical finite trace, a trace may be non-normal or
unbounded, and a von Neumann algebra may require a weight or predual. Keep:

    state / CP / GNS                  general
    finite trace pairing              finite-dimensional
    trace class / predual             analytic specialization

### A.10.4. CFC is not a complete entropy API

Continuous functional calculus handles continuous functions on spectra. Log at
zero, support restrictions, and some entropy formulas need care. Use positivity
and support projections to define finite formulas safely, and do not imply that
the general C⋆-algebra layer already supports every entropy expression.

### A.10.5. Bounded versus unbounded observables

The current dynamics is bounded C⋆-algebra dynamics. Position, momentum, and
many physically important Hamiltonians are unbounded. They need a distinct
operator-domain/strong-continuity development. Keeping this boundary explicit
will prevent misleading definitions and impossible typeclass requirements.

### A.10.6. Tensor products

Mathlib has algebraic star tensor constructions, but a turnkey spatial C⋆
tensor-product theory is not the same as finite Kronecker products. The finite
layer can use matrices and Euclidean spaces immediately. A general tensor
product API should be introduced only with a deliberate choice among algebraic,
minimal/spatial, maximal, and Hilbert-space tensor products.

### A.10.7. Normality and infinite systems

Quantum information often uses density operators because finite-dimensional
duality is especially clean. In infinite systems, normal states and normal CP
maps on von Neumann algebras are often the right setting. The C⋆ layer should
remain useful without normality, while a future VonNeumannAlgebra/predual
layer can provide the stronger infinite-dimensional semantics.

## A.12. Immediate implementation queue

The highest-value next tasks are small enough to be done incrementally:

1. Add a Channel.lean or refine Basic.lean so the unital CP channel has
   composition and identity.
2. Add a state constructor and state pullback along a unital positive map.
3. Factor the GNS code out of State.lean into a reusable representation file.
4. Add a finite theorem explicitly identifying the new density operator type
   with the old MState fields.
5. Audit and localize matrix/operator order instances.
6. Port finite POVMs to the new Effect type.
7. Port one representative channel theorem from QuantumInfo through
   CompletelyPositiveMap, preferably a Kraus-to-CP direction.
8. Repair the two current build blockers: qubit Lie names and the dynamics
   kernel theorem.
9. Add deprecation adapters before moving entropy and resource-theory clients.

The first concrete vertical slice should be:

    abstract state
      → GNS/vector state
      → finite density operator
      → matrix expectation
      → one finite POVM/channel theorem

That slice will validate the abstraction boundary much more effectively than a
large top-down rewrite.

## A.14. Bottom line

The project does not need to choose between general operator algebra and useful
finite quantum information. It needs a one-way dependency architecture:

    C⋆-algebra concepts
      → Hilbert/GNS representation concepts
        → finite trace/matrix concepts
          → quantum-information algorithms and inequalities

The existing abstract state/GNS and finite density-operator work means the most
difficult conceptual bridge has already been started. The replacement should
preserve that work, make mathlib's positive/CP/CFC APIs authoritative, and
move QuantumInfo results behind finite adapters. Once channels and states are
expressed this way, measurements, dynamics, entropy, and eventually resource
theory can share one operator-algebra backbone without forcing general
C⋆-algebra users to import finite matrices.
