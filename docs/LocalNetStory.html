From local measurements to infinite periodic systems

The common starting point is a net: each finite region has observables and states, and including one region in another tells us how to compare measurements.

This lets us build the infinite system from its local pieces. Periodicity then organizes its observable sectors by momentum; equilibrium comes from taking limits of finite-volume Gibbs states. The examples below use the same framework.

What the examples actually show

Independent classical spins

Product states, explicitly

With no coupling and a uniform field, Gibbs probabilities factor into one-site probabilities. Their marginals agree exactly, so the infinite-volume state is the explicit product state. This instantiates a general product-state construction for any finite spin alphabet.

Classical Ising chain

An interacting equilibrium example

Nearest-neighbor couplings are translation invariant. An exhausting family of Gibbs states has a thermodynamic limit that gives a global state through the general net construction. We have not proved uniqueness, a transfer-matrix solution, or translation invariance of the chosen interacting limit.

Two-component periodic waves

A simple band picture

A sector of actual spin observables is grouped into cells with two components. A translation-equivariant restoring-force operator has branches λb(k) = mb + κ(2 − 2 cos k). These are squared wave frequencies. The example instantiates general unit-cell and Bloch constructions; the two components are uncoupled.

Quantum longitudinal Ising

Thermal identities in the limit

The observables are full matrix algebras; the interaction terms commute. Complex-time evolution of a local observable stabilizes within a finite neighborhood. This instantiates a reusable theorem that passes finite-volume thermal identities to every thermodynamic limit state satisfying the limit hypothesis.

How powerful is the KMS result?

For local observables A and B, we construct an entire correlation function F with the thermal boundary relation: F(t) gives ⟨A αt(B)⟩ and F(t + iβ) gives ⟨αt(B) A⟩. The limit theorem is independent of the particular Ising couplings and exhausting family, provided its exact local analytic evolution assumptions hold.

To reach the standard KMS theorem on a completed observable algebra, we still need norm completion, bounded-strip control, and extension beyond local observables. General noncommuting interactions also require a broader dynamics construction and propagation estimates. A transverse field is outside this exact-support example. Classical Gibbs equilibrium needs its own DLR formulation.

What lands in this one PR

Include

  • The net, states, symmetries, and algebraic local-to-global construction.
  • Thermodynamic limits and their general compatibility results.
  • Momentum sectors, unit cells, and Bloch reduction.
  • The four examples above, plus the general local thermal-limit theorem.
  • Physics-focused documentation, imports, and references.

Leave for later

  • General causal dynamics, no-signalling, and composition of systems.
  • Spacetime nets, time-slice/additivity applications, and Minkowski diamonds.
  • A full spectral decomposition and coupled band examples.
  • Completed-algebra KMS and general noncommuting dynamics.

Locality here means observables organized by support and consistent inclusions. The PR does not claim a universal dynamical-locality theorem. Likewise, an ultrafilter thermodynamic limit does not by itself prove convergence of the whole volume family.

Where the implementation lives

LocalNet carries the framework; Crystal carries momentum/Bloch results; SpinLattice supplies classical and quantum nets, products, waves, and the thermal-limit theorem; IsingModel supplies Gibbs states and concrete applications. A small complex-extension utility supports the quantum expectations.
